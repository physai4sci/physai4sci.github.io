About

AI for science is moving beyond predicting properties and recommending experiments. AI systems are beginning to interact with the physical laboratory: choosing experiments, controlling robots and scientific instruments, interpreting measurements, and using feedback to decide what to do next.

PhysAI4Sci brings together researchers in machine learning, robotics, scientific agents, self-driving laboratories, autonomous instrumentation, and experimental science. Our central question is: How should AI systems learn, reason, and act when their decisions change the physical scientific world?

Unlike purely digital environments, laboratories involve slow, costly, noisy, and sometimes irreversible actions. Experiments may produce images, spectra, diffraction patterns, or biological assays rather than simple rewards. The goal is not only optimization, but reliable scientific discovery.

Research Themes

We welcome work spanning chemistry, materials science, biology, and scientific instrumentation, with an emphasis on transferable machine learning ideas.

  • Planning and control for physical experimentation. Scientific agents, hierarchical planning, tool and instrument grounding, multi-agent coordination, physical constraints, failure detection, and recovery.
  • Measurement and characterization in the loop. Active sensing, multimodal state estimation, autonomous microscopy and spectroscopy, adaptive acquisition, and deciding what and when to measure.
  • Learning from autonomous experimental histories. Experimental trajectories, robot and instrument logs, failed runs, protocol changes, offline learning, and reusable representations of physical workflows.
  • Provenance, attribution, and security. Auditability, experimental traceability, process fingerprinting, integrity of records, and lab-of-origin attribution.
  • Generalization, evaluation, and scientific validity. Scientific world models, digital twins, cross-instrument transfer, reproducibility, causal validation, and benchmarks for real-world discovery.

Call for Papers

We welcome new, ongoing, and preliminary work on learning, reasoning, and acting through physical experiments. We are particularly interested in scientific agents, autonomous experimentation, adaptive measurement, experimental histories, provenance, reproducibility, cross-laboratory generalization, and evaluation.

Two non-archival submission formats are planned. Page limits exclude references:

  • Regular research papers — up to 8 pages. New methods, experimental results, scientific systems, benchmarks, and evaluations.
  • Short papers — up to 4 pages. Preliminary ideas, position papers, datasets, benchmarks, system demonstrations, and analyses of negative results or failed experiments.

Negative results and failures are especially encouraged. We welcome reports of failed experiments, instrument errors, recovery events, incomplete execution traces, and lessons from real laboratory constraints that are often missing from traditional scientific datasets. Previously published research may provide context, but contributed submissions should present new work or analysis.

Submissions will be managed through OpenReview and will receive three independent reviews. AI-generated submissions are not permitted under the stated ICLR 2027 policy; AI-assisted writing is allowed for primarily human-authored work. All reviewing and acceptance decisions will remain with human reviewers and organizers.

The workshop submission link and formatting instructions will be provided after workshop acceptance. We are also coordinating with PRX Intelligence on a potential themed collection for subsequently developed full journal manuscripts, subject to standard editorial and peer review.
